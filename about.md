  <div class="post-content">
    <p>I'm Vasu Gupta, an analog and power IC designer at Analog Devices, where I work on low-noise DC-DC buck converters for RF, OLED, and automotive rails.</p>
    <p>The quickest way I know to understand a circuit is to follow its currents: where charge moves, which nodes respond, where energy is stored or dissipated, and how feedback closes the loop. Once that physical picture is clear, the equations stop looking like algebra to memorize and read directly as statements about the circuit.</p>
    <p>The <a href="{{ '/#projects' | relative_url }}">projects</a> span switched-inductor power stages, noise-shaping data converters, floating-gate device modeling, and analog sizing — each laying out the central design bottleneck, my part in the work, and what came from modeling versus silicon. The <a href="{{ '/#tools' | relative_url }}">tools</a> let you vary a component or operating point and watch the waveforms respond in real time.</p>
